\section{Overview}

This report presents a function-level profiling analysis of the
\textbf{Falcon} post-quantum digital signature scheme
(NIST PQC Round 3 finalist, lattice-based).
Two parameter sets are compared: \textbf{Falcon-512} (NIST Level~1)
and \textbf{Falcon-1024} (NIST Level~5).
Each operation was executed $\mathbf{1000}$ times and profiled with
\texttt{gprof} under two compiler settings:
\texttt{-O0 -fno-inline -pg} and \texttt{-O3 -fno-inline -pg}.

Functions are grouped into three categories:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \colorbox{hashcolor}{\strut Hash / PRNG} ---
        SHAKE-256 (Keccak), PRNG refill, hash-to-point.
  \item \colorbox{intcolor}{\strut Integer Arithmetic} ---
        Modular NTT (\texttt{modp\_*}, \texttt{mq\_*}),
        multi-precision integers (\texttt{zint\_*}),
        Gaussian sampler (\texttt{mkgauss}).
  \item \colorbox{fpcolor}{\strut Floating-Point / FFT} ---
        Custom 53-bit FPR type (\texttt{fpr\_*}, \texttt{FPR}),
        FFT/iFFT, polynomial operations.
\end{itemize}

\section{Key Findings}

\begin{itemize}

  \item \textbf{KeyGen is dominated by Integer NTT and FP/FFT arithmetic.}
        \texttt{modp\_montymul} (Montgomery multiplication for NTT)
        and \texttt{fpr\_add} (FFT floating-point add) together
        account for over 40\% of total keygen time.
        The lattice basis generation (\texttt{zint\_*}, \texttt{mkgauss})
        contributes a further $\sim$10--15\%.

  \item \textbf{Sign is almost entirely FP/FFT.}
        The \texttt{ffSampling\_fft\_dyntree} recursive sampler
        drives repeated calls to \texttt{fpr\_add} ($\sim$50\%)
        and \texttt{fpr\_mul} ($\sim$15\%).
        Integer NTT (\texttt{mq\_montymul}) is visible but minor ($<$2\%).

  \item \textbf{Verify is entirely Hash and Integer NTT.}
        No floating-point FFT is used. Time splits between
        \texttt{hash\_to\_point\_ct} (SHAKE-256 hashing of the message)
        and \texttt{mq\_montymul} / \texttt{mq\_NTT}
        (NTT-based polynomial multiplication over $\mathbb{Z}_q$).
        This is why verify is 70--300$\times$ faster than keygen.

  \item \textbf{Falcon-1024 costs $\approx 2$--$3\times$ more than Falcon-512.}
        The FFT and NTT algorithms are $\mathcal{O}(N \log N)$;
        doubling $N$ from 512 to 1024 roughly doubles the cost
        with an additional $\log$ factor.

  \item \textbf{O3 gives 4--5$\times$ speedup on keygen/sign,
        7$\times$ on verify.}
        The \texttt{FPR} constructor overhead drops from $\sim$15\%
        to $<$5\% under O3 (compiler promotes the struct boxing).
        Verify benefits most from O3 because \texttt{mq\_montymul}
        vectorises well.

\end{itemize}
